Purpose of the site

This website is a catalog of 3-ball juggling tricks that I have found. Some are classic tricks, like the box and the shuffle. Some I have discovered online. Most I have invented myself.

Each trick is created from combining basic building blocks -- a single throw and catch -- in sequence. What makes each building block unique is the placement and movement of the hands and balls. The charts represent how different building blocks can be combined to form tricks.

Hopefully this website can teach you some new tricks, or inspire you to create new ones.

Siteswap

Siteswap is the most common juggling notation. Siteswap gives each juggling trick a code, expressing which balls pass through which hands at which times. There are many tutorials available online, like this one explaining traditional siteswap and this one explaining asynchronous siteswap.

We can generate an infinite number of tricks just by playing with the siteswap. However, each siteswap itself has many different ways it can be performed, depending on the placement and movement of the hands and balls. But there's always a "simplest way" to perform a siteswap: the hands remain uncrossed below all the balls, with each beat in the siteswap following a constant tempo. Juggling animator websites (like this one) will demonstrate this for you. Just plug in a siteswap and it will, by default, show you the simplest way to perform it.

Beyond Siteswap

However, like I said, there are other, more fun ways to juggle. That's where the notation fails, and I come in. Each chart on the website corresponds to a single siteswap; I've focused on the most common 3 ball siteswaps. I break the siteswap into its constituent steps, which are given in bullet points. I explore different variations of how each step may be performed -- not just the "simplest way". I've given each variation a (hopefully intuitive) name -- for instance, "Roll" and "Take-out" are a couple variations of the (2,4x) step, with "Roll" being the "simplest way." These variations are shown in red on the first row and column of the chart. The cells, then, represent tricks obtained when the variations are combined in sequence.

Click on a cell in the table marked with a "✔" to see a video of me performing the trick, as well as my diagrams and notes on that particular trick. If there's an "X" on the cell, it means I haven't been able to perform the trick or it's just not humanly possible.

Happy juggling!
